**TL;DR - In an age where attention is scarce, a research post earns its keep by giving readers the whole idea in three minutes or less.**

Long-form essays used to signal rigor. Not anymore. Readers arrive from a search result, a share, or a link; if they cannot see the shape of the argument within the first paragraph, they leave.

The rewrite is not to write less, but to distill more. State the finding up front. Support it in the middle. Close with what the reader should do.

Three habits we hold research posts to:

- **One-sentence TL;DR at the top.** If the whole point cannot survive that compression, the post needs more thinking, not more words.
- **Four to six paragraphs, no more.** The constraint forces choices. Which detail earns space, which does not.
- **A single takeaway a reader can act on.** If they cannot cite what to do next, the piece was descriptive rather than useful.

The payoff is not just for readers. Writing to this shape sharpens my own thinking, every draft is a compression pass.
