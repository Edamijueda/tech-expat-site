**TL;DR — In an age where attention is scarce, a research post earns its keep by giving readers the whole idea in three minutes or less.**

Long-form essays used to signal rigor. That trade no longer clears. Readers arrive from a search result, a share, or a link; if they cannot see the shape of the argument within the first paragraph, they leave.

The rewrite is not to write less — it is to distill more. State the finding up front. Support it in the middle. Close with what the reader should do differently. Everything else is scaffolding to remove.

Three habits I now hold research posts to:

- **One-sentence TL;DR at the top.** If the whole point cannot survive that compression, the post needs more thinking, not more words.
- **Four to six paragraphs, no more.** The constraint forces choices — which detail earns space, which anecdote loses out. Constraints improve prose.
- **A single takeaway a reader can act on.** If they cannot cite what to do next, the piece was descriptive rather than useful.

The payoff is not just for readers. Writing to this shape sharpens my own thinking — every draft is a compression pass, and compression exposes where the argument was thin. A three-minute post that survives revision tends to be more honest than a thirty-minute essay that hides its gaps in volume.
